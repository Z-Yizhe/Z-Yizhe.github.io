## 1.basic definitions
consider the equation：

$$
P(x,y)dx+Q(x,y)dy=0    (2.15)  
$$

if  $P(x,y) and Q(x,y)$  can be expressed as the product of X and Y(X--function of x,Y--function of y),then we call such an equation a **separatable differential equation**.
So it can be written in the following form:  

$$
X(x)Y_1(y)dx+X_1(x)Y(y)dy=0         (2.16)
$$

if $X_1(x)=Y_1(y)=1$,then it is a exact differential equation

$$
X(x)dx+Y(y)dy=0.        (2.17)
$$

And the general integral is:

$$
\int X(x)dx +\int Y(y)dy=C        (2.18)
$$

Most of the time, it is not as simple aas that,but if we divide both sides of the equation by the factor X_1(x)Y_1(y):  

$$
\frac{X(x)}{X_1(x)}dx + \frac{Y(y)}{Y_1(y)}dy=0        (2.19)
$$

it turns into exact differential equation,so we can find its general integral easily:

$$
\int \frac{X(x)}{X_1(x)}dx + \int \frac{Y(y)}{Y_1(y)}dy=C     (2.20)
$$

But we can not confuse the methods for solving equation(2.16) and (2.19) ,as $X_1(x)Y_1(y)$ might equals 0

so the real answer should be:

$$
\begin{cases}
\frac{X(x)}{X_1(x)}dx + \frac{Y(y)}{Y_1(y)}dy=0 ,\\
X_1(x)=0,\\
Y_1(y)=0.
\end{cases}
$$
